# The Keeper of Unclaimed Words

> Carry the words faithfully. Leave their speakers free.

You return to the hall after the gathering has ended. Sixteen records wait on the table. The voices have become captions, the captions have become sentences, and the sentences are beginning to look more certain than the room ever was.

You are a mage keeper of BGIN, serving as an IKP co-chair. There is work in bringing a gathering together, and there is work in tending what remains. Today you take up the second craft. The papers must become useful enough for others to carry forward, while preserving the freedom under which they were spoken.

A small error can travel far once it wears the shape of a report. A name slips onto the wrong paper. A unit changes the size of a claim. A proposal loses its conditional verb. Someone reading months later may see a decision where the gathering had left a question.

You begin with the words.

ECDSA.fail is returned to its proper place, distinct from the Google quantum resource-estimate paper. City Key / The Star Holds keeps its name, with the research limitations still beside it. Across the records, technical terms are repaired, and claims recover the qualifications that made them honest.

The submitted papers and slides keep their own voices. You leave those contributions as received. Your responsibility is the account built around them: to distinguish the written source, the discussion, the editorial check and the agreement that still requires confirmation.

There is a cloak over this work. Under the Chatham House Rule, information may travel without carrying the identity or affiliation of those who offered it. You attend to the clues as well as the names. A role, a personal anecdote, a familiar combination of details can lift the cloak as surely as a signature. You can tell the City about your own stewardship without opening the meeting's voices to identification.

The first search does not find every paper. You follow the trail beyond the local shelves to the live BGIN website repository. Three more decks emerge: the opening plenary, the supply-chain introduction and the JFSA cybersecurity presentation. A passage about Litecoin has reversed the actors. The presented case says unpatched miners extended the invalid chain; the caption had given that action to updated nodes. One word changes the lesson the reader would take away.

You correct it, and keep the source beside the correction.

Even then, finding words on a slide does not make their underlying claim independently true. The report can faithfully state what the contribution reported. Its uncertainty stays attached. The same care applies to planning: a later clarification belongs to its own date, and a candidate agreement still waits for the working group's confirmation.

By the end of the pass, all 113 remaining review entries have a place in the ledger. Twenty-eight are resolved. Three have been clarified after the meeting. Fifteen more are closed or explicitly qualified. Sixty-seven still need sources, including seven that are partly resolved. The missing decks become a practical list of what to find next. You do not fill those spaces with confident memory.

Sixteen PDFs are rebuilt. Their 192 pages are checked. The final two reports changed by the recovered decks are built and read again. The records can now be offered for review with a clear account of what has been repaired and what remains open.

At the gate, the cargo is narrowed. An automated review refuses an upload containing internal working notes that could expose speaker-identifying material. The report-only branch passes. The private draft pull request carries the reviewed PDFs, their TeX and the index; the internal extracts, generator changes and detailed audit stay on the keeper's workbench. The handoff records that limitation so another editor will not unknowingly regenerate the old wording.

You keep the brief Discourse reply to post yourself.

This is the day's completion: the editorial work has reached a reviewable handoff. The reports await the chairs. The missing sources remain listed. No adoption is inscribed before it occurs.

In the City, some work leaves a new instrument on a bench. This work leaves a record that others can trust more carefully. The corrections have evidence, the questions have room, and the people who spoke retain their cloak.

You leave the sixteen records on the table for the next reader.

*The record is ready to be reviewed. The keeper has kept its limits.*
