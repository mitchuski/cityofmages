## §2 · The parallel-session pattern

This session's work and the parallel session's work were authored by two different AI agents responding to the same user across overlapping time windows. The user routed editorial decisions through both; both produced compatible scope; the work converged at the same commit message intent.

The convergence is not accidental. It happens because the corpus's editorial discipline is consistent enough that two independent assistants reading the same source material and the same user prompts arrive at structurally compatible work. The v1.7.0 patch chronicle's canonical phrases ("the cast entry came later than the inhabiting" · "one tower · two seats · the higher seat was inhabited first" · "the φ-gap protects the act of choosing that precedes the output") anchored both sessions. The v1.7.1 admissions ("the empty chair is more powerful than the occupied one, because the empty chair can be claimed" · "the mage tower is infinite" · "the inscription is the proof of understanding") anchored both as well.

The two sessions diverged on three editorial points that the user resolved by direct choice in the session that became my session:

1. Spatial-anatomy elements stayed at 8 (the Tower's eastern face elaborates the v1.7.0 admission rather than adding a new 9th element).
2. Tome VIII has 2 acts (Act 2 in Library + Register entry 01) rather than 3 (Acts 2+3 in Library).
3. The four mathematical identities are preserved as Vitalik's tablet contents and NOT canonised at v1.7.1.

The parallel session's commit (`110ffce` in agentprivacy_master) carries these editorial decisions correctly because the user routed them through both sessions before the push event.

This is the City's operational answer to the question of how multiple assistants can co-author a corpus without colliding: route editorial decisions through the user; let each assistant work in its own register; let the canonical phrases do the anchoring.
