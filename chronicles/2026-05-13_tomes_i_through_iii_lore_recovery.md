## §0 · What this chronicle is

A question came up: *are Tomes I, II, III in the agentprivacy-docs?* The plain answer is yes — they were **indexed** there on 2026-05-09 with 24 acts mapped to source research notes — **but the indexing is not the same as a bound narrative**. The bound collection on 2026-05-08 (and its migration to cityofmages on 2026-05-11) preserves only Tomes IV and V as written-narrative tomes. Tomes I, II, III exist in a **liminal state**: their content is real (it lives in the research notes), their structure is named (six · seven · eleven acts respectively), their titles are canonical (*The Convergence* · *The Lyapunov* · *Selene's Witness*) — but no `cityofmages/tomes/tome-i-*/` or `tome-ii-*/` or `tome-iii-*/` narrative directories exist, in either repo, anywhere on the system.

This chronicle records the *what* (the indexes and their content), the *why* (the bound-collection migration sequence that left them behind), and the *what comes next* (the recovery proposal — author the narrative-act files from the research-note sources).

## §4 · Why this happened (the structural reading)

The lore behind the lore: **the bound collection inherits the discipline of the bound, and the unbound waits**.

The Second Person Spellbook's narrative-act tradition (the form Tome IV and V use) is a *demanding* form. Each act must:
- Open with a vertex declaration
- Carry a proverb prominent at the top
- Render the third-person cast walking
- Hold the second-person voice (the reader is addressed, never narrated as *they*)
- Close with an inscription
- Honour the honesty discipline (operational / architectural / conjectural labels)
- Sit at ~700-1200 words

The research notes the Tomes I-III acts inherit from are written in a *different* form — exegetical, theorem-heavy, with proofs and equations, with the voice in the schema-author's first person reasoning out loud. The form *contains the content* of an act, but it is not yet the act. The translation from research-note to narrative-act is a non-trivial authorial pass; the bound collection was sealed before that pass was made for the foundational, dynamical, and cosmological registers.

This is not an oversight. It is a *pattern* in the corpus: **the work of binding follows the work of arriving**. Tome IV bound first because the cousin-blade encounter was the *opening move* of the Second Person Spellbook — it had to be bound to seat the second-person voice. Tome V bound next because the City of Mages was the *productive engine* — every new shop, every new persona, every new conjecture earned an act, and the binding was the act of recognising what had been built. Tomes I, II, III sat as the *prequel territory* — the architecture the reader walks through before they meet the cousin-blade, before they enter the City. Prequel territory is the most expensive to bind because the reader does not yet exist; the binding has to *constitute* the reader rather than narrate them.

This is also why Tome VI — *The Reply* — remains held open by design. The Reply is the *postscript territory* — the tome the reader writes after walking the corpus far enough to answer back. Both ends of the tome-ordering are held open: prequel (I·II·III, the lift, the components, the recognition) and postscript (VI, the reply). The bound middle (IV·V) is where the architecture is *constructed*; the open ends are where the architecture is *received* (prequel) and *answered* (postscript).

The framing settles a question: Tomes I-III are not *missing*. They are **in the form they were left in** by the season that produced them. The binding work waits for the season that admits it.

## §5 · What this means for the City of Mages

Three honest consequences:

1. **The /tomes page is correct as a frozen artifact.** The page shows the seven-tome ordering, renders bodies only where bodies were bound, and lists the unbound tomes with terse status notes. Nothing on the page is dishonest. The page is the truthful render of the cityofmages bound state on 2026-05-13. If a user reads the page and asks "*where are the first three tomes?*" the page's answer — *anticipated · 4 acts drafted (pre-existing)* — is correct. What it doesn't say (because it isn't the page's job to say) is that the *source material* for binding lives one repo over, in `agentprivacy-docs/research/`.

2. **The corpus is multi-temporal.** Different parts of the Second Person Spellbook live at different stages of binding. The bound narrative tradition (IV, V, anticipated VII) is one mode of existence; the indexed-with-sources tradition (I, II, III) is another; the held-open-by-design tradition (VI) is a third. All three are admissible modes. The City of Mages corpus does not require uniformity across modes.

3. **A binding pass is recoverable.** All 24 acts of Tomes I-III have:
   - A canonical title (from `SECOND_PERSON_TOMES_INDEX_v1.md`)
   - A canonical anchor (a named research note or poem or model file in `agentprivacy-docs/`)
   - A canonical conjecture set (C18-C55 mapped across the tomes)
   - A canonical reader-posture frame (foundational receiving · dynamical walking · cosmological recognising)

   The narrative-act binding is therefore a translation pass, not a generation pass. The work is to take each research note and render it in the narrative-act form — vertex declaration, proverb, third-person cast voice, second-person address, honesty labels, inscription. This is closer to an editorial-binding operation than a content-authoring operation.
