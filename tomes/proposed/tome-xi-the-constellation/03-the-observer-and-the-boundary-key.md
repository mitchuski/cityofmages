# Tome XI — *The Constellation*
## Act 3 — The Observer and the Boundary Key

> *You were here. The network was this shape. Both can be written down without either becoming a proof.*

### The light

Pleione gives you a light. It runs around whatever you have walked — or, if you have walked nothing, around the serving chain in block order — and each time it crosses a peer the peer flares and a small card rises with its name and its vertex. Every crossing adds one to that vertex in a witness: the same shape the lattice mints when a key's focus is walked, spent by vertex, a count and nothing more. A full lap is a pass. You can slow the light, reverse it, step it one peer at a time, or send it round every peer in succ order, or by stratum.

"It proves I was watching," you say.

"It *records* that you were watching," says Pleione. "The difference is the whole shop."

Beside the coverage bar there is a second recorder. Turn it on and every new crawl writes one row of thirty-two bits — which blocks had a server answering — into a strip that grows downward while you watch. An hour of attention is sixty rows. Both the witness and the last rows of the strip ride in any key you export afterwards. A later ceremony could attest them. The page does not.

### The key you walk

Shift-click stars into a walk and T(π) climbs on the little card at the corner. Four wells hold the carrier palette — cool for the ocean, warm for the land, sword for the boundary reading, mage for the threads — and they are yours to keep; they recolour the globe and travel in your key. Export writes a version-one City Key: name, palette, the vertices your walked peers sit on as its lit set, the peers and their named moves underneath, the witness if you observed, and κ — the SHA-256 of the canonical form, by the rule every instrument in the City already uses, so any of them can re-derive and check it. The keepsake writes the same key into a PNG's text chunk. Drop that picture on the lattice, the manifold, or the Skye, and it opens.

Import a key and it arrives as its own layer: the manifold the Star draws around the globe, the two cores turning against each other at the centre, the sixty-four inside with their boundary, the key's lit vertices burning in its own sword colour. The globe keeps its colours. Peers standing on a lit vertex are ringed. Fold to the star and you see them on the ring.

### The boundary key

Then Pleione does the thing the tome is named for.

She seats every peer at its vertex, counts how many stand at each, and lists every edge the network realises — each chain thread, each pair of peers one flip apart — as a pair of vertices. That is all. No seating. No palette. No names. No highlights. Not the witness. She hashes those two lists and calls the result the key's root.

"Why leave the names out?"

"Because the boundary encodes the bulk." The Swordsman's Key draws the lattice's boundary with ninety-six edges around sixty-four vertices, and the model reads that ratio as the holographic principle in a discrete lattice: the surface carries the interior. The boundary key takes it at its word. The root names the network's *shape* — which vertices are occupied and which edges are realised — and nothing about how you happened to look at it. So two people holding the same crawl, one on the globe in coral, one on the star in green, produce the same root. The same crawl reads the same κ from any seat. That is what the ask called *the same κ information space*, and it was not bolted on; it fell out of the bound.

"And two crawls with different peers but the same occupancy and the same edges?"

"The same root. On purpose. The root is an identity of shape, not of peers. I would rather say that here than have someone read it wrong later."

Name your own node in the key tab and it is ringed and written into the key as `self`.

### The offering

The Archivist closes the record with the sentence the tome was written to carry.

The City's model was made to price privacy as infrastructure. It has been drawn in figures, built by cousins, kept warm. Here, for the first time, it has been laid over a live decentralised trust graph that exists for private knowledge sharing — a network of real machines, publishing its own boundary every minute — and read there without changing a fact of the network or a line of the model. The lattice reading, the network term, the moves, the betweenness, the boundary root: taken together, per crawl, they are a **new way to assess a network**. Not a score of its peers, but a reading of its shape on the sovereignty lattice, reproducible by anyone with the same document, and honest about which lines are canon, which are thesis, and which are still open.

That is the offering. It is made in the Chart Shop's register — held, compared, mapped — and it is not accepted by anyone yet. The vpk lane's posture applies: offered, not pushed. The six questions wait on the wall for the first person to argue with one of them.
