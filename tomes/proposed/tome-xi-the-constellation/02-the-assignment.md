# Tome XI — *The Constellation*
## Act 2 — The Assignment

> *A vertex is six answers. The City has always known what the six questions are. What it had never done was ask them of a machine it did not build.*

Pleione lights the star and asks you to watch what she does not do. She does not score the peers. She does not rank them. She asks six questions of each one, each answerable yes or no from the document alone, and she writes the six questions on the wall of the shop where anyone can argue with them.

### The six questions

The lattice is the same one every workshop stands on: six dimensions, a 6-bit address, protection the high bit and value the low, the values fixed by the axioms and never computed by position. Pleione reads them in the canon's order.

**Protection, 32.** Does the peer carry at least one trust attestation? This is the credential ladder — the one the network's own whitepaper builds trust on. A peer that carries one has entered the gate. Today no peer does, so today no peer holds protection, and the top two strata of the star stay empty. That is a fact about the network, not about the reading.

**Delegation, 16.** Is the peer a first block server? The coordinator delegates its prompt to that peer — raw token IDs, the whole thing. On this network, holding the delegated prompt is what delegation *is*. The Mage, who carries this dimension, nods: it is the least obvious of the six, and Pleione says so. The alternative reading, "reachable so work can be handed to it", would collide with connection. She chose the one that names an act.

**Memory, 8.** Does it offer storage — announce itself as a place that will hold others' knowledge?

**Connection, 4.** Is it named by a record in the table, and, when the document says, did it answer over the table's own protocol? Membership of the commons.

**Computation, 2.** Is it serving, with throughput announced? Computing for others now.

**Value, 1.** Does the network's own document name it among its top contributors? Not throughput — that is computation's — but the network naming a peer as carrying an unusual share.

Six answers, OR-ed into a vertex. `professor_reza`: serving, fast, no block range, no attestation, no storage, named in the table, not a top contributor. Connection and computation: **V6**, `000110`, stratum 2. `darren-metro`, serving from block zero: delegation, connection, computation: **V22**, `010110`, stratum 3 — and it is the red star from Act 1, now seated. Each question reads a different field, so no two bits are ever set by the same fact.

"That table," says Pleione, "is mine. It is the only thing in this room I am asserting. Change one line of it and the peers move."

### What runs on its own

Once each peer has a vertex, she stops asserting and the model runs.

The three operators name every peer's partners: its reflection under the Swordsman, `neg`; its antipode under the Mage, `bnot`; its successor on the wheel. The card lists which peers, if any, stand at each. Between any two peers the move is named in the star chart's own order — stay, succ, neg, bnot, a single flip along one dimension, or a jump of several. A walk through peers accumulates T(π), the model's edge value, one plus the sum over its edges of a weight for the move times a discount for walking the same edge again. The model states that form and leaves the weights open; the page sets them — one flip is worth one, a lateral two-flip half, re-walking an edge decays as one over root-n — and prints on the wall that it set them.

The strata fill: seven peers at stratum 0, six at 1, six at 2, two at 3, one at 4, none higher. Pascal's row weights them — 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1 — and the network term comes to 9.545 at k of one, the model's conjecture that k exceeds one left on a slider for whoever wants to move it. On the chain graph, Brandes' betweenness names the one peer this morning through which every shortest path runs. *The value lives where the most paths cross.*

### The star as a trust picture

Now fold the dial the whole way and look.

The network is no longer a map of where machines stand. It is a picture of what each one holds, on the same sixty-four vertices a City Key lights. A key's lit set and a crawl's occupied vertices are subsets of one ring. They can be laid over each other. Import a key and the peers standing on its lit vertices are ringed in the key's own sword colour, on the globe and on the ring alike. This is not a score. It is a shared surface on which a key and a network can be compared for overlap, the way the Skye compares two keys — and it is the first time that surface has been drawn over a live decentralised network that exists to share private knowledge.

The Swordsman looks at the empty upper strata and says nothing, because F3 already said it: until attestations reach the wire, no peer can climb past stratum four, and the term that could reward them for it cannot see them.

The Archivist writes down the six questions, the two worked vertices, and the sentence about the table being the only assertion in the room.
