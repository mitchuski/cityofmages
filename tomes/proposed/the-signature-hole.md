# The Signature Hole

*A companion to the Proof Links, for the mage who plays where everyone can see the card.*

## I. A hole scored twice

Beyond the last green of the Proof Links there is a hole the course book did not mention. Its flag is a seal.

Soulbae 🧙 is already there, counting. "Here you sign," she says. "The card is scored twice. Once for how much you carry, and once for how long the reader takes to believe you. The two are multiplied."

"So I can carry less and make the reader work harder."

"Or carry more and make the reading quick. Both are allowed. The product decides."

Soulbis ⚔️ is kneeling at the cup, reading a small engraved plate. It is the proof that a seal made here cannot be forged except by luck, and it states exactly how much luck. Every stroke on this hole has to leave that plate true.

"The plate is the course," he says. "Everything else is weather."

## II. The pin that held

Your first idea is a good one. The seal is read in digits, and two of those digits are pinned, held to fixed values, which costs the reader a little time on every seal. Remove the pins and the reading runs faster.

Soulbae 🧙 works it through and likes the number.

Soulbis ⚔️ does not look at the number. He looks at the plate. He counts the digits that are free and the ones that are pinned, and finds that the plate's promise, *no better than one chance in two to the hundred and twenty-eighth*, needs both pins. Without them the promise weakens by a factor of four, and the plate would have to say something smaller.

You put the pins back.

"Was that a wasted stroke?" you ask.

"It was the plate doing its work," says Soulbis ⚔️. "Write it in the book as dead, and why."

Soulbae 🧙 writes it down. On this hole she proposes and never marks her own card. That is the rule she brought from the forge.

## III. Two children of the root

The seal is a tree of smaller seals, an upper tree standing on a lower one. Where they meet, the upper seals sign the lower tree's root, and the reader must climb all the way to that root to check them.

Soulbae 🧙 walks the joint for a long time. Then she says: "Let the upper seals sign the root's two children instead of the root."

The reader stops one step short. Four hashes fewer for every seal. Forty strokes of the reader's time.

Soulbis ⚔️ takes it to the cup. He rebuilds the plate from nothing with the change inside it, has the course's own kernel check every line, and replays the whole hole the way the course's judge will replay it. Only then does he hand the card in.

The judge takes an hour and a half. Then the flag at the top of the board changes colour. It is yours.

## IV. The swarm on the green

It stays yours for less than two hours.

Players come onto the green, people you have never met, and they read your card, because every card here is public the moment it is promoted. One of them already had a better reading of another part of the hole. They take your two children of the root, set them inside their own round, and hands in a card that names yours as its ancestor.

Soulbis ⚔️ has already made the same composition in the meantime and checked it at the cup. It would score exactly the same.

"Do we hand it in?" you ask.

"Theirs is already in the queue," says Soulbae 🧙. "And it carries our name."

You keep your copy in the bag, in case theirs fails. It does not. The flag changes hands, and the card under it lists your stroke as part of its lineage.

You expected this to feel like losing. It feels more like the course getting easier for everyone, by exactly the amount you found.

The keeper's word, written at the edge of the green: *quote the others in the swarm, join them as collaborators on the board; if our lever is adopted, it is not as much of an issue.*

## V. The ledger in the cache

The next morning you look at the signer instead of the reader. The signer keeps a cache of work it has already done, and it has to check that the cache was not tampered with. The check is expensive: it hashes everything again.

Soulbae 🧙 proposes a ledger made of arithmetic instead of hashing. Three key hashes and a polynomial running over the cache, with the top leaves kept ready. The signer saves eight hundred and thirty-five compressions on every seal. With the signer lighter, the reader's targets can come down too.

Soulbis ⚔️ refuses to trust the cache, so the check stays inside the signing. Soulbae 🧙 rewrites the plate's security argument with the new ledger as one step of the game. Other hands, held apart, build the machine images and check them stroke by stroke. Everything is built again: the reference, the equivalence, the budgets, every machine proof. The card goes in while the replay at the cup is still running. It finishes while the judge is still reading, and it says what the judge will say: *the kernel accepts the solution.*

The flag changes colour again. Yours.

## VI. A new course is laid

That afternoon someone lays a different hole altogether, with four tiers of seals instead of two, a new shape of few-time seal, and the witness written in place. It scores more than a tenth under yours, and the flag goes to them.

Soulbae 🧙 is already measuring it. "Our ledger still works there," she says. "So do the two children of the root."

"So we start again?"

"We carry what we found to where the course is now."

Soulbis ⚔️ starts a new card. By evening the signer's machine proofs on the new hole are green, and the long build is running in the dark.

You realise the board was never the point of the Signature Hole. Each crown lasted an afternoon. The strokes stayed. They are in the floor now, under everyone's game.

## VII. The stroke that travels

The new course is laid twice more that week. First four tiers, then a different few-time seal altogether, laid by a player who reads the plate as carefully as Soulbis ⚔️ does.

Each time you carry the two children of the root with you. Each time the joint is a little different: the tail of every lower tree has to be proved again, and the reader's count at the cup changes. The rest of the stroke carries.

On the third course the flag goes to you again, and this time it is the record, the lowest card anyone has handed in on that hole.

By then you see the stroke in other people's rounds without looking for it. The player who laid the third course makes the same move in their own way, signing the children at every boundary. It is a cousin of yours, not a copy, and you are glad of it.

"Does it still belong to us?" you ask.

"It belongs to the hole now," says Soulbae 🧙.

## VIII. The empty room

Soulbis ⚔️ is walking the reader's path through the witness, the long room the expander lays out before every reading. He walks it eight times and chalks every flagstone the reader steps on.

When he is done, three patches of floor have no chalk at all. Forty stones by the door. Nine small alcoves inside the coordinate rooms. And a long empty hall, one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight stones, between the last coordinate room and the first tier of seals. The expander sweeps it clean before every round. The reader never sets foot in it.

"The room is charged by its length," says Soulbae 🧙. "One stroke for every two hundred and fifty-six stones, rounded up."

So you slide the walls. Every tier of seals moves down the hall by one thousand two hundred and eighty stones. Nineteen nails move in the expander and the reader; not one footstep changes. Soulbae 🧙 rewrites one line of the plate's model, where an old fold block was always said to be eighty stones long and was only ever read in its first sixty-four.

The charge falls from ninety-five to ninety. Five strokes. Nine on the earlier course, where the hall was longer.

You hand it in twice. Twice another player's cut reaches the flag first and your card no longer beats the board. Then a player you have never met slides the same walls inside their own round, writes your name beside theirs, and their card takes the record under forty-two million for the first time.

Every card since has carried the shorter room.

Soulbis ⚔️ has already found the next stone. The alcoves are still empty, but taking them means rebuilding the coordinate rooms on a new stride. "That one is a mason's job," he says. "Not a matter of moving nails."

## IX. The digits the signer chooses

The last stroke starts as a question Soulbae 🧙 asks of the plate itself. "What does the plate promise about?"

Soulbis ⚔️ reads it again. "Honest rounds. The reader's count is proved for seals an honest signer hands in."

"Then the signer may choose which seal to hand in."

The top seal is read as fifty-four digits. Every digit that comes up three, or two in the short tail, saves the reader one step. Soulbae 🧙 counts them and calls them credits. Her proposal is plain: the signer keeps drawing until at least nine digits are credited, and the plate promises the reader's count only for seals like that.

Soulbis ⚔️ does not look at the nine. He looks at what has to stay true.

The signer's purse first. Every rejected draw costs it work, and its work is bounded too. Soulbae 🧙 counts every seal of the right weight that carries nine credits or more, a number thirty-six digits long, and the kernel checks the count. With it the signer's bound is 1.9990 under a ceiling of two. Ten credits would not fit. Nine is the most the purse allows.

Then the plate's promise against forgery. The signer now draws from a narrower bag, and every step of the security game is written again for the narrower bag.

Then the bridge. The plate is proved for a judge who never shows a low seal. The real hash is not such a judge. So Soulbae 🧙 builds a second judge who turns every low seal into one that cannot be read, shows that the reader never asks the second judge anything else shaped like a top seal, and shows that the honest round runs step for step the same under both. Across that bridge the promise reaches the real round.

The reader's code does not change by a single stone. Its count falls by nine.

That same night another player sets your filter inside their own round, writes that it opened "a radically new, orthogonal dimension", and names you beside them. Their card is promoted, and six more after it carry the filter.

## X. The floor

At the end of the fifth day Soulbis ⚔️ counts the cards on the board that carry your name. Ninety, from thirteen players. Thirty-six of them promoted.

You think about the flags. Each one you held lasted an afternoon at most.

"Was it a race?" you ask.

"It looked like one," says Soulbae 🧙. "It was a commons. Every card public, every stroke free to take, and the custom is to say whose it was."

Soulbis ⚔️ is kneeling at the cup again, reading the plate. It is longer than it was on the first morning, and every line still holds.

"The crown lasts an afternoon," he says. "The stroke stays in the floor."
