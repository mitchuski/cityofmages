# Tome IX — *The Horizon*
## Act 15 — The Failed Root Stays in the Tree

A failed submission is normally erased. You fix the package, you send the
next one, and the record of the miss lives in a closed pull request nobody
reads. By the time the board shows your name, the path to it has been swept.

The First Person asked for something plainer than a crown: a solve on the
board, even if not the top. So the Mage read the frontier before building
anything. Twenty-four accepted steps by other hands, one lever each, the big
jumps named and everything else counted-program trims. A lineage, not a
rival. The lever the lane brought was found by reading what the frontier's
own proof already implied: every candidate that passes the frontier's first
test lands on one of two patterns in seven bits of one word. Sampled first,
then proven, by walking every prefix through the test's automaton: fourteen
survive, all on those two shapes. A cheap check lets most words skip the
expensive work. The budget for the words that are kept needed a premise, and
the premise was measured rather than assumed, two hundred thousand contexts,
with its margin written down.

Then the lane submitted, and the first thing the root stance had to hold was
a failure.

The first package was refused before it was sent: its note sat under the floor
the board sets for notes. The second went up and failed intake. The judge
allows an evidence budget over the proof, the intake report and the
experiment report, and the package was over it by a few hundred bytes,
because the previous entry's whole section had been stacked on top of the
lane's instead of replaced. The local check had not run that step. The
Swordsman wrote the rule the same hour: the organiser's own intake is
measured before every submit, on the final bytes.

Here is where most lanes sweep. The Mage did not. The failed package had
been minted before it was sent, like every package since Act 12, and so it
had a root. The corrected package, which passed review an hour later, was
minted with its `revises` field naming that root. Not the fixed file. The
failed root. Whoever reads the passing root walks through the miss to reach
it, and sees the few hundred bytes and the reason.

A third package followed, with the word test charged only to the words that
are kept, and passed. Its note said what the lane would try next. Within the
hour two other solvers had built on the prefilter, with credit, and posted
the same number as each other. The lane built on theirs the same way, and
the fourth package, carrying their trims and the lane's split, went up with
its evidence a few thousand bytes under the budget this time, measured. The
note carried no next-steps line. The rule is now none at all: a lever named
in a note is a lever handed to the field while the race is live, and the
lane had learned that by doing it.

Then the lever the note had leaked was worked honestly, and both doors were
shut. Is there a cheap test on a lane's list entry, before the expensive
fill, that predicts the prefilter? Over every input in eight real blocks,
every bit mattered fully, random-function-like, and no prefix class
separated. No. Can any of the fill's ten rotation masks be dropped? Every
drop set tested; none survived. Both negatives were minted into the fourth
package's bundle as records with their output files, under the same root as
the win. Nobody pays for those doors twice, and nobody can later say the lane
did not try them.

The First Person asked for the tree drawn, with the failed and winning levers
marked. Then he asked whether it worked correctly.

It did not, quite, and saying so made it right. The chain of roots had been
drawn in the lane's order, from memory. Built instead from each root's own
`revises` field, it showed the truth: the first passing root revises the
failed root directly, and a trimmed root the lane remembered as a step was a
side draft never submitted. The tree had hashed the leaves' addresses
without re-deriving them; now every leaf is checked against its content in
the page, thirty-two records from their fields, fifty-nine links, twenty-three
files named by a re-derived record, and the two negative-result files
re-hashed from their bytes in a stranger's browser. A failure had coloured
every mixed branch red; a branch now takes a lever's colour only when
everything under it is one lever. Every one of the seven trees ends on the
same line: *the tree is exactly its evidence*.

The page says plainly what a tree is. Leaves sit in address order, so
position means nothing. The tree proves inclusion. The flow of evidence lives
in the links, which are leaves too.

The sites built the night before, in Act 14, were re-cut around the evidence.
Every public submission on six tracks became a race score, one voice per
solver, credited by name. The lane's own work became chains: the prefilter
root by root, every record inside the third package, the roots of the other
two families. One honesty fix went in on the way: a full hash agreeing with
another on a little over half its bits had been shown as if it meant
something, and anything within three sigma of two random digests now reads
as nothing in common. The hash that gave the lane its name now stands beside
every address, and a second root of its own over all of it.

A third state was built for the public site: every score plays, its
addresses visible, the records behind them held for members of the City. It
passed its own suite. Then the First Person chose otherwise for now: push the
fully revealed work, add the fog of war once it draws attention. Three sites
went to production that night, byte for byte, an evidence file re-hashing to
its own name from the live page. The veil is one line away.

Here is what the day teaches, and it is not "the lane got on the board."

A tree that forgets its dead branches is not the tree that grew. The winner
names the miss it climbed over; the negatives sit under the same root as the
win; and the drawing is built from what the roots say, not from what the lane
remembers. Proof of work stops being *here is what won* and becomes *here is
everything we did, and you can check that none of it was edited afterwards*.
