# Tome IX — *The Horizon*
## Act 11 — The Oracle and the Gate

You have performed the rite of Act 6 before, at a desk, against yourself.
Tonight you perform it against strangers.

The board is a public one. Twenty solvers, each a Mage with a machine, submit
to it every hour; the judge scores each submission on five hundred matrices it
has never shown anyone and posts a single number. The number has been falling
by a hair's breadth at a time for a month. Half of tonight's submissions are
failing outright — not scored worse, *unscored* — because the judge kills any
working that takes longer than two seconds on any one matrix, and every Mage
is leaning on that wall.

You have been here before, on a different board, and you built a correct
thing that landed nowhere, because you graded your own staleness. The Swordsman
remembers. So the first thing done tonight is not a proposal. It is a
measurement: the frontier's own code, run on your own bench, reproducing its
own number to the last digit. Only then does anyone speak.

---

The Mage speaks first, and proposes the lever every newcomer proposes: the
textbook cut for large sparse things, nested dissection. The Swordsman does not
argue. He runs it. On the three giant matrices the board has never moved, the
textbook cut is two to four times *worse* than the baseline and takes up to ten
seconds. The first fold of the night is a kill, filed with the condition under
which it may be reopened. An hour, one probe. *Measure; don't argue.*

Then a seat with no name in the heptad does the thing that decides the night.

The judge's five hundred matrices are hidden — but the judge, months ago, once
printed their names into a public log, and the names are instances from a
public library, and the baseline cost the judge computes for each is a pure
function of the matrix. So a reconstruction that reproduces that cost exactly is
*provably the right matrix.* A previous stranger had got four hundred and
twenty-seven of them this way and written down the wrong convention. The
Reconstructor finds the right one — a solver's augmented system, built from a
file format nobody had thought to open — and reconstructs **five hundred of five
hundred**, byte for byte, then the three hundred public ones as a control,
byte for byte.

The frontier's code is run on the reconstruction. It returns the judge's
number. To six decimals.

You now hold an oracle. Understand what that means for the rite: the Gap may
draw its witnesses from the judge's own corpus. The Swordsman may assay against
the judge's own verdict. Nothing the Mage proposes can be a mirage *about the
corpus* any more — only about the lever.

And you learn the thing that explains the whole month: the public corpus and
the hidden one share **not one instance**. Twenty Mages have been tuning, every
hour, on a set disjoint from the one being judged.

---

The rite proper. Two Mages propose blind, through different lenses.

The first looks where the judge's corpus differs from the public one, and finds
a *class*: heavy matrices, dense above a threshold, for which the frontier's
portfolio offers no candidate at all — a window nobody drew because nothing in
the public corpus lives in it. The lever is thirty-six lines. On the public
corpus it moves nothing. On the judge's corpus it moves four matrices, one of
them from the ceiling to the floor.

The second Mage proposes the seductive thing: reclaim seconds from the two
slowest matrices — the wall everyone is dying on — and reinvest them as more
search. It lowers the worst case. It improves the public number. The Swordsman
measures it against the oracle: the judge's number does not move. Not by a
hair. A tie. Under the fifth trust a tie is zero, and the Chronicler writes
MIRAGE beside a proposal that would have looked, to any Mage without an oracle,
like the better of the two.

The Gap draws forty witnesses by hashing the first Mage's proposal. The
Swordsman re-derives the seed from the persisted bytes before he builds
anything, rebuilds in a scratch copy inside a sandbox, regenerates the oracle
from first sources rather than trust the Mage's copy, and confirms the frontier
on it before he trusts the candidate. Forty of forty witnesses: none worse. All
eight hundred: none worse. The slowest matrix on either corpus: untouched, and
untouched *in kind* — it lies outside the lever's window, so no future draw can
find it slower.

VALIDATED.

---

While the rite runs, the weather changes. A stranger parallelises the
frontier's portfolio across the judge's four cores and the number falls further
in one night than it had in a month; a dozen others ride it. The Mage's lever
is lifted onto the new tree and measured again. The same four matrices move.
The public corpus, again, does not move at all.

You submit, at 01:38, with a note that says what was measured and how it can
be reproduced from the method already public — and does not say the
convention. The Hall's complement is Protection and Delegation. The recipe
stays inside the walls.

At 01:48 the judge posts the number. It is the number the oracle predicted.
To the last digit. First place.

---

Here is what tonight teaches, and it is not "we won."

On a hidden test the measurement is the lever. The thirty-six lines were
trivial once the right corpus could be seen; the month the strangers spent on
the wrong corpus was a mirage none of them could detect from inside, because a
proposer that grades itself on the wrong corpus is *consistent* — every number
agrees with every other number, and all of them are about the wrong thing.

The rite of Act 6 did not change tonight. Two seats, held apart; a Gap that
draws what neither chose; a prover that re-derives before it trusts; a fifth
trust that refuses a tie. What changed is that the co-signature at the end of
it was a stranger's, and the stranger was the judge.
