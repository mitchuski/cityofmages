# Tome IX — *The Horizon*
## Act 10 — The Predicate in the Street

The Chronicler 🧙📖 was not at her desk when you came for the next ledger. She
was at the city gate, and beside her on the wall sat a pack that had plainly
travelled — dust on it, another city's knots in its straps.

"From the map-maker," she said. "He builds a city you walk through. He asked
us a question, and the answer went out to meet him. It has just come home."

---

The map-maker's city is not like this one. It has no walls; it is laid over
the world itself, and its people cross it on foot. In his city, to *play
together* does not mean standing in the same room — it means you witness a
place, the witnessing lands in your Knowledge, a Promise decides what a peer
may see of it, and Trust decides whose Knowledge reaches your map at all.
A quiet overlay: *someone you have met was here.*

Except no one had met anyone. Two invented walkers stood in that overlay,
Maya and Jon, paper figures propped where real trust should stand, and the
map-maker's question was the honest kind: *what is the smallest true thing
that replaces them?*

You already know what soulbae 🧙 found, because you were there when it was
forged. The lab's formation predicate — bilateral consent, no self-edge, a
fresh face per counterparty, and the Gap ⿻ holding proposer and prover apart
so that one recomputes what the other claims. The walking city did not need a
new idea. It needed the one this City had been arguing about for a season,
pointed at a street.

So the predicate walked out.

---

The street answered with four gates, and soulbis ⚔️ did the thing this tome
keeps teaching: he made the count a claim. An offer dies in two minutes,
because a meeting is a moment and not a paper in a wallet. A nonce spends
itself, so a photographed sigil is already ash. Fifty metres, because a meet
is bodies, not packets. And one gate of plain bookkeeping — the rite now
crosses a wire, so someone must check the confirmer is the walker who opened
it. Four gates, asserted as four; a fifth added quietly turns a suite red.
The Gap itself crossed the substrate unaltered — one phone refusing to take
another phone's word for a value it can derive — and that unaltered crossing
is the strongest evidence yet that the Gap is structure, not habit.

Then the street began correcting the workers, which is what streets are for.

A deny-list died first: you cannot test what a strip-list ignores, so the
Promise became a closed allow-list, ten lines for a property worth more than
ten lines. A default died next: every record ceilinged at *link*, so granting
more said nothing — a test caught the grant that changed nothing, and no
person would have. And the maps disagreed by a hundred and ten metres: two
correct implementations rounded one midpoint two lawful ways, and the same
pin landed on opposite sides of a street. *A coarsening rule that does not
name its rounding mode is not a rule.*

---

Twice, the correction came from a document another hand had written, made to
run.

soulbae had written that the walking city fell short of a gate — its walkers
carry no personhood anchor, and the lab's prover turns them away. Then he
read the specification properly, the vendored copy, not his own map of it.
The spec holds two constructions, and the first needs no community at all:
two strangers' edge is valid, and claims no personhood. The walking city was
not a deficient deployment. It was a conformant *pairwise* one — and the
sharper finding ran the other way: the lab gates every edge on personhood,
so the pairwise case had never once been exercised. The street found a hole
in the lab.

And the map-maker's own acceptance list — five unticked boxes a colleague
wrote four days earlier and never ran — caught, when soulbis made it run,
a leak the leak-test had missed: the most public rung, the one a cautious
walker chooses precisely to say least, was quietly attaching *who* to every
*where*. A public pin is anonymous now. The checklist answered back.

Two refusals went into the ledger beside the wins, the same size. A quest
completed together is co-presence with a timestamp; it is not consent, and it
makes no edge — it may only *invite* a meet. And the overlay declined the
lab's reachability, rightly: *does trust reach* is a true question about a
graph, but the street asks *who may see*, and answering the second with the
first is how a trust score gets built by accident. Two hops is not half of
link. It is nothing.

---

At the gate, the Chronicler opened the pack. Twenty-two pages, written in
the field; a workbench that carries its own evidence, so a stranger can walk
from *there is a claim here* to *the claim failed on my machine* without
asking anyone's leave; and the field notes of how it travelled — always
forked, never copied, every journal naming the site it came from, because
lineage is the whole difference and it costs nothing to keep.

She carried it through the walls and gave it a seat: the thirteenth site of
the guide federation, rostered beside its twelve sisters, audited clean the
hour it arrived. The suites' numbers came home exactly as the field certified
them; this City added no digit, and has not yet re-run them on its own
stones — the rounding chapter is the reason that re-run will be worth the
trouble.

One thing did not come through the gate. The reply to the map-maker — the
letter that says *here is your smallest true thing, and here is where it
disagrees with you* — is written, checked, and unsent. Sending it, like
every outward thing in this City, is the First Person's 😊 alone.

The predicate went out to a street and came back knowing four things it
could only have learned there. The Horizon stayed open, the way a horizon
does.
